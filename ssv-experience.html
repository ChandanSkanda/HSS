Sangh Shiksha Varg Experience

What SSV Taught Me Beyond the Schedule

Some experiences teach you skills. Others quietly change the person you become.

When I registered for Sangh Shiksha Varg (SSV), I expected to learn discipline, physical activities, and gain a deeper understanding of Hindu thought.

I knew the days would be long and structured, but I didn't realize that the biggest transformation would happen in my mindset.

Looking back, there wasn't just one challenge that I overcame. There were many small moments that changed the way I think.

The Unexpected Freedom of Giving Up My Phone

One of the first things that happened was that our phones were collected.

Honestly, I thought it would be difficult. Like most of us, I have developed the habit of reaching for my phone every few minutes without even realizing it. Not because I needed it — but simply because it had become automatic. Checking Slack messages, emails, notifications, or just unlocking the phone for no reason had become part of everyday life.

But something unexpected happened. Instead of feeling disconnected, I felt… peaceful. There was no pressure to check work messages. No fear of missing something. No constant stream of notifications competing for my attention.

It made me realize that many of the small stresses we experience every day are self-created. We become so used to carrying them that we stop noticing them. The calmness started from that very first day.

A Lesson in Leadership

On the first day, we reached the venue late. Jignesh Ji called us aside and scolded us. For a moment, it felt like being back in college, standing in front of a strict principal.

But after the conversation ended, I noticed something interesting. None of us felt insulted. Instead, we genuinely felt guilty for being late.

There is a huge difference between making someone feel bad and making someone realize they made a mistake. Jignesh Ji never tried to prove that we were wrong. He simply helped us realize it ourselves. That was leadership.

It actually made me think, I wish more teachers had corrected us this way during our school days. People don't change because someone raises their voice. They change when they know the person correcting them genuinely cares.

Feeling Like Family

That feeling became even stronger afterward. Despite us being late, everyone waited for us. They made sure we settled in comfortably before continuing.

I kept asking myself, "Who would care this much for people they barely know?" That is when I understood one of the biggest strengths of HSS. It doesn't just build volunteers. It builds people who naturally take responsibility for one another.

Responsibility isn't taught through lectures. It is demonstrated through actions.

Finding Answers… and Learning to Ask Better Questions

I came to SSV carrying many questions about Hinduism. One question stayed in my mind for a long time: Hindu Dharma naturally fits into everyday life in India because the culture around us supports it. But how does it apply when we live outside India?

I wondered whether simply following the traditions passed down by our families was enough. Through Bauddhik sessions and Charcha, many of these questions started finding answers. Not all of them. And honestly, I'm glad they didn't — because I realized SSV doesn't expect blind acceptance. It encourages thoughtful discussion.

I still question certain practices, and I hope I'll understand them better in future Vargs. For me, that openness to ask questions made the learning much more meaningful.

Excitement Can Beat Laziness

If you ask anyone who knows me, they'll tell you one thing. I am terrible at waking up early. At home, waking up around 7 a.m. itself feels like an achievement. Before that? Almost impossible.

At SSV, we were sleeping around midnight after a full day of activities, yet somehow waking up around 5:30 or 6 every morning. Surprisingly, I never felt frustrated. Why? Because excitement became stronger than tiredness.

Sometimes we don't lack discipline. We simply lack something exciting enough to wake up for.

The Bond That Cannot Be Explained

Yesterday I met a few swayamsevaks from Saranga Shakha. Watching them together, I assumed they all lived nearby. I asked them, "So… do you all stay in the same neighborhood?" "No." "Same school?" "No." "Same college?" Again, no.

Yet they interacted like childhood friends. Some of them meet only once every few months. Some meet only once every few years. Still, the warmth between them was incredible.

It reminded me of my own experience. I had met a few people during Yuva Varg for just two days. Two months later, when we met again at SSV, it honestly felt like reconnecting with old friends. Not because we had spent years together. But because we had shared meaningful experiences together.

That feeling is difficult to explain. SSV has a unique way of bringing together people from different cities, backgrounds, professions, and age groups, yet making them feel like one family. Someone from San Diego can instantly connect with someone from Dublin as though they've grown up together. That bond is something truly special.

What I'm Taking Home

When I came to SSV, I expected to learn discipline. I am leaving with much more.

A calmer mind. A new appreciation for disciplined living. A better understanding of leadership. Many answers. A few new questions. And friendships that I believe will last a lifetime.

If someone asks me what the biggest challenge at SSV was, I'd say it wasn't waking up early or following a packed schedule. The biggest challenge was stepping outside my comfort zone. And that's exactly where the biggest growth happened.

I could probably talk about my SSV experience for hours. But for now, I'll end with one thought:

Some experiences teach you skills.
Others quietly change the person you become.
For me, SSV was one of those experiences.
